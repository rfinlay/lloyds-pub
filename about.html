Hats, shirts, and whatever else

Selling merch through the site

You asked whether the site could sell merchandise. It can. Here's how I'd set it up, and why.

Start with buy online, pick up at the bar

Someone orders a shirt from their phone, pays, and picks it up at Lloyd's. The reason to start here is simple: a person who comes in for a shirt usually buys a drink while they're there. The store feeds the bar, not just the store.

It's also the easier version to run. Nothing to box up, nothing to mail, no postage to figure out. A bartender hands over a shirt and checks a name off a list.

Add shipping later, as a switch, not a rebuild

A bar that's been open since 1978 has people who moved away and still think of it as theirs. Somebody in Phoenix who drank there in 1994 wants that shirt and can't drive over for it. Shipping is how that person buys it.

The kind of store I'm describing has shipping built in already. Turning it on later means setting a shipping rate and deciding who takes the box to the post office. It doesn't mean rebuilding anything. I'd get pickup running first, then turn shipping on once pickup is working and you've decided you want the extra step. It's worth turning on eventually. It just isn't where to start.

What goes in it

Hats and shirts to start, since those exist. Whatever else you carry goes in the same way. I don't know what's on the shelf right now, and that's one of the things I'd want to see rather than guess at.

How the store actually works

The store runs on an established platform, something like Square or Shopify, wired into the site. It isn't software I'd build myself. That's on purpose, and it means a few things:

  • The account is in your name. Money from sales goes straight to your business. Refunds and chargebacks sit with your business too, which is where they belong.
  • You add a product from your phone. Take a picture, type the name and what you charge for it, tap save. There's no admin system to learn.
  • The store keeps working regardless of who built the site. If I got hit by a bus tomorrow, your store would still take orders on Friday.

The honest tradeoff

The platform charges a monthly fee and takes a small cut of each sale. That's the cost of the store being something you own, that handles card processing and the security around it, and that keeps running no matter who built the site. I'd rather say that now than have it show up later as a surprise.

One thing worth checking: Square

Does Lloyd's already run Square at the register? I don't know, and I'd want to before pointing at any one platform. If it does, adding online sales is a smaller step, because the products and the payouts already live in a system you log into. If it's something else, or a plain card machine, the other options work fine. It just changes which one I'd recommend.

After it goes live

What happens after launch

Three ways this can go. The first one is what happens on its own at handover. It isn't something you sign up for. The other two sit on top of it, if you ever want them.

One

You own it and run it yourself Default

The site is plain files in a hosting account in your name. The store is your own account. Nothing renews, nothing is owed, and nobody's permission is needed to change anything.

This is where you land by doing nothing.

Two

Call when it's easier not to

Something needs changing and you'd rather not deal with it: a new photo, updated hours, a section reworded. You call, I quote it before any work starts, you say yes or no.

This sits on top of the first option. It doesn't replace it.

Three

A standing arrangement

Regular updates on a schedule. Worth mentioning for a bar specifically: a bar has real weekly content, live music, specials, game day, in a way most businesses don't. For most places I wouldn't bring this up. For a bar it can earn its keep.

None of the three has a number on it here. The numbers come after we've talked and I know what you actually want. The first option is the default and costs nothing. Do nothing, and that's where you are.

Before any numbers

What I'd want to ask you

Not a form. Just the four things that change what I'd recommend.

  1. Does the bar run Square, or Toast, at the register already? This decides which store platform makes sense.
  2. What merch actually exists right now? Better if you can show me than tell me.
  3. When new shirts come in, who adds them to the site? You, Harley, a bartender, or me.
  4. Is there a logo file anywhere? Or does the logo only exist on the sign and a t-shirt.

A short sit-down at a slow time is easier than email for all of this. Whenever works for you.
